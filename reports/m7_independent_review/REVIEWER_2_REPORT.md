Written as a reviewer trying to reject the paper. Each objection is classified after weighing the evidence from this audit.

| # | objection | class | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leakage is already well known. | **MAJOR** | True (Barz & Denzler, Laroca, Tampu, automotive work). The paper survives only if it is framed as a *controlled measurement in a new domain* plus a design contribution (matched test set, probe/control, exposure dose-response). It cannot be framed as discovering the phenomenon. |
| 2 | +1.76 mAP50-95 is too small. | **MODERATE** | Overall it is small, and with seed uncertainty its interval touches zero (−0.01 to +4.05). The paper should lead with exposed items (+3.9 pp; strongly exposed +6.6–7.4 pp) and present the overall number as "benchmark-level optimism is modest". That is itself a useful, honest result, and it agrees with large-scale dedup reports. |
| 3 | Three seeds are insufficient. | **MAJOR** | With df = 2 the probe and probe − control intervals include zero. D1 and D2 have one seed each, and D1 has no C1 replicate. The replication across designs and the dose-response partly compensate. Two to four more runs would fix this cheaply. |
| 4 | Training noise is too large. | **MAJOR** | Same-condition RMS on subsets is 1.76 pp (probes) and 2.71 pp (probe − control), i.e. one seed's contrast is about 1.5σ. Mitigations: all 12 cross-condition model pairs give positive probe gains (min +1.55), and strongly exposed probes gain consistently across seeds (6.21 / 7.91 / 6.28). Must be reported with subset-level noise, not the 1.13 overall floor. |
| 5 | D1 contradicts the clean story. | **MAJOR** | Controls moved +3.09 with no bug, no exposure and no composition cause. Placebo p = 0.35. The plausible explanation (training offset) is supported by one replicate pair but not proven. Not fatal: strongly exposed D1 probes still stand out (+7.4 vs +3.1). It does rule out "only exposed items move" as a general claim. |
| 6 | DINO grouping is arbitrary. | **MAJOR** | Visually, NEAR_DUPLICATE pairs are 29% duplicate-like and 59% related; cross-source links are 0% related; PCB-Defect family links are 6% related. The groups are noisy. The paper must say "machine-defined group-mates (mostly same-family)" and not "near-duplicates". |
| 7 | DINO-small/base sensitivity undermines grouping. | **MODERATE** | Discrete components barely overlap within DsPCBSD+ and PCB-IND (ARI ≈ 0.01), and 43% of probes lose their exposure label under base groups. But the continuous exposure contrast and the dose-response hold under base. This undermines the *group labels*, not the *effect*. |
| 8 | There was no human validation. | **MAJOR** | Still true. An AI review of 300 pairs plus a 90-pair probe–mate check is informative (κ 0.82, controls separated cleanly), but it is one model labelling twice. A blinded two-person human review of at least 100–150 pairs is required before any venue that cares about data claims. |
| 9 | AI visual review is not human validation. | **VALID, MODERATE** | Agreed. It must be reported exactly as "AI visual adjudication", with its limits (same model both passes; 5–20% misses on low-texture synthetic duplicates). It is useful as a pre-screen and as a falsification test of the "near-duplicate" wording, which it falsified. |
| 10 | Single model architecture. | **MAJOR** for a full paper / **MODERATE** for a workshop | YOLO11n only. Memorisation of group-mates could scale with capacity in either direction. One larger detector (e.g. YOLO11s/m, or an RT-DETR) on D0 is the obvious addition. |
| 11 | Single domain. | **MODERATE** | PCB only. Acceptable for an industrial-vision workshop; limiting for a general CV venue. |
| 12 | PCB datasets are too niche. | **MINOR** | The venue decides. For industrial inspection audiences the domain is the point. |
| 13 | Source identity confounds B results. | **MODERATE** (as stated) / **NOT VALID** as a criticism of the leakage result | Correct that a size/format probe identifies the source at 100%, and that B mixes acquisition domain with everything else. The project already says B measures source/domain shift and does not compare it with C0/C1. The wording must avoid "unseen defect semantics". |
| 14 | Probe/control construction may itself encode difficulty. | **MAJOR** | Probes are grouped items and controls are mostly singletons (AUC 0.98 from group size alone). Composition differs: controls hold 2× PCB-Defect and fewer short boxes. The within-subset contrast removes level differences but not interactions. Partial rebuttals: D0 grouped controls did not gain (−0.99), covariate-adjusted per-image models keep the probe effect, and the dose-response *within* probes is not a probe/control artefact. |
| 15 | Bootstrap CI is incomplete. | **MAJOR** (now addressed) | The reported CI ignored seed variation. A hierarchical bootstrap (groups + seeds) gives overall −0.01 to +4.05 and probe − control −0.68 to +8.46. The paper must report this or an equivalent, not the narrower interval. |

## Summary as Reviewer #2

> The authors show, in a carefully built matched design, that YOLO11n gains a few mAP points on PCB test images whose group-mates appear in training. The engineering is excellent and the point estimates reproduce exactly. However, the "near-duplicate" framing is not supported by visual inspection: most exposing mates are same-family crops, not duplicates. The primary interval omits seed variance and becomes borderline when it is included. One of three designs (D1) shows control movement of similar size. Only one architecture is tested. I would **reject for a main CV venue** and **accept for a data-centric / industrial-vision workshop** conditional on: (a) hierarchical uncertainty, (b) reframing around graded group exposure, (c) a small human validation of probe–mate pairs, and (d) clearer separation of the source-shift result as the practically dominant one.

No objection is **FATAL**: none shows the effect is an artefact. Eight are **MAJOR** (#1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 14, 15), plus #10 for a full paper. All of them can be addressed without changing the experiment's core; #15 has already been addressed in this review.
